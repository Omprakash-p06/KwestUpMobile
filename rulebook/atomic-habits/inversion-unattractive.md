# The 2nd Inversion — Make It Unattractive

> "Every behavior has a surface-level craving and a deeper underlying motive. Your habits are modern solutions to ancient desires. When you can reframe your habits to highlight their benefits rather than their drawbacks, you make them more attractive. Inversely, highlighting the immediate negative costs makes bad habits unattractive." — James Clear

## Philosophical Principle

A craving is simply the realization that something is missing. We associate a bad habit with a quick fix to an underlying emotional state (e.g., smoking to relieve stress, scrolling social media to cure boredom).
To break a bad habit:
1. Reframe your mindset: consciously associate the bad habit with negative feelings and trade-offs.
2. Highlight the benefits of avoiding the bad habit (e.g., financial savings, mental clarity, restful sleep).

## KwestUp Implementation

### 1. Cost & Trade-off Reflection
- When logging or reviewing negative habits (e.g., spending, excessive screen time), KwestUp links domain insights from other modules:
  - If a user logs an impulse purchase in `BillingContext`, the app can show how it impacts their monthly savings target.
  - If phone usage disrupts sleep, the check-in engine highlights the morning fatigue correlation.

### 2. Reframing Prompts in Weekly Reviews
- During weekly reviews, the system prompts the user with factual friction insights:
  - "When you stayed up past 23:30, your workout completion the next morning dropped by 75%."
  - This factual juxtaposition makes the late-night behavior unattractive without resorting to moralizing or guilt.
